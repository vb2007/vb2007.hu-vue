# Design System: vb2007.hu

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Route Board"**

The site is a metro line diagram. A light green field is the ground; white enamel sign panels carry content in dark green ink behind a 2px edge; one mid-green route line ties the pieces together. Tools that exist are solid stops on the line, tools that are coming are dashed stops with an amber hatch. The mood is civic, legible, tactile and cheerful, like station wayfinding rather than a developer dashboard.

Density is moderate: a 78rem page, generous 3rem section gaps, large condensed uppercase headings, and body copy at 17px. Depth comes from physical metaphors (enamel thickness, pressable keys), not from blur or glow. Rejected by the direction: the dark-terminal homelab dashboard and the rounded-card SaaS page.

All tokens are declared once with CSS `light-dark()`; the `light-theme` / `dark-theme` class on `<html>` only switches `color-scheme`. Dark theme keeps the same hue family (about 152) with a deep green field and the same panel-on-field relationship.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Green-tinted everything: every neutral carries hue 130-152, there is no gray.
- Condensed uppercase station-board headings against humanist Barlow body.
- Hard-offset, pressable buttons; 2px ink edges on panels and fields.
- Solid line = open, dashed line + amber hatch = planned.
- Reduced-motion collapses all animation and transition durations.
- Icons are stroked 24px line glyphs; brand marks (GitHub) are the one filled exception, drawn on their own grid.

## Colors

A restrained-plus palette: a light green field dominates, white-green panels sit on it, and a single mid-green line plus a deep green action colour do all the accenting. Amber appears only as the "works in progress" hatch.

### Primary
- **Route Green** (`line`): the line itself. Strip connectors, stop-marker rings, focus outlines, link underlines in tickets, the navbar underline bar, footer-less route accents. Never used as text on the field (contrast), only as stroke.
- **Signal Forest** (`action`): action and link colour. Primary button fill, board header, current-stop fill, text links, success text, selection. Deeper variant `action-deep` is the button's hard-offset underside.

### Secondary
- **Works Amber** (`works-amber` with `on-works` ink): paired amber and dark-amber diagonal hatch, used only for the kerb stripe under board headers and planned-stop signalling.

### Syntax
Code in a paste is coloured like the lines on a metro map: each kind of token rides its own line colour, restrained like signage ink. Every syntax colour keeps at least 5:1 on Enamel White and Sunk Enamel in both themes (`--c-syn-*` in `src/styles/tokens.css`).
- **Keywords** take Signal Forest at weight 650; **comments** take Soft Ink in italic.
- **String Clay** (`syn-string`): strings, regexes, links.
- **Number Blue** (`syn-number`): numbers and literals.
- **Title Plum** (`syn-title`, weight 600): function, class and section names.
- **Type Teal** (`syn-type`): types, built-ins, `this`/`self`.
- **Attribute Ochre** (`syn-attr`): attributes, properties, meta (decorators, preprocessor). A dark text ochre, never the amber hatch.
- Diffs: added lines in Signal Forest on Confirm Wash, removed lines in Fault Red on its wash.

### Neutral
- **Meadow Field** (`field`): page background and ticket-slip fill. Dominant surface.
- **Deep Meadow** (`field-deep`): hover wash on ghost buttons and strip stops, and the enamel-thickness underside of panels.
- **Enamel White** (`panel`): sign panels, navbar, secondary buttons, focused inputs.
- **Sunk Enamel** (`panel-sunk`): input fill, disabled buttons, info messages.
- **Board Ink** (`ink`): body text, panel edges (the 2px `edge`), the footer band in light theme.
- **Soft Ink** (`ink-soft`): hints, placeholders, planned-stop labels and dashes.
- **Hairline** (`hairline`, ink at 20%): row dividers inside boards, the empty rail of progress tracks and meters, the edge of disabled buttons.
- **Terminal Band** (`deep` / `on-deep`): footer, deep green in both themes, with the line recoloured bright green on it.
- **Fault Red** (`danger`, `danger-wash`) and **Confirm Wash** (`ok-wash`): error and success message tints only.

Dark-theme counterparts live in `src/styles/tokens.css` and the sidecar; frontmatter records the light values.

### Named Rules
**The One Line Rule.** There is one route colour. It is a stroke (lines, rings, focus), not a fill for large areas; large fills belong to the field or panel.
**The Green Neutral Rule.** Neutrals are tinted toward hue 130-152. Pure white, black and gray are not used.
**The Hatch Means Planned Rule.** The amber diagonal hatch and dashed strokes mean "not built yet" or "work in progress", and nothing else.
**The Route Measure Rule.** Anything that measures (upload progress, storage, a share of a total) is a stretch of line: a hairline rail, a route-green run along it, stop rings at the ends. Fault Red marks a failed run or a full terminus; Soft Ink a cancelled one.
**The Line Colours Rule.** Syntax colours are metro line colours, one per kind of token, kept at 5:1 or more on enamel and sunk enamel in both themes. They colour code only, never UI.

## Typography

**Display / Headline / Label Font:** Barlow Condensed (600, 700) with Barlow, system-ui
**Body Font:** Barlow (400, 500, 600) with system-ui
**Code Font:** JetBrains Mono Variable, for URLs and the mono input variant

**Character:** Condensed uppercase signage over a friendly humanist text face; the mono is reserved for literal strings the user will copy.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (700, `clamp(3rem, 1.5rem + 7vw, 6rem)`, 0.92): the home hero title only.
- **Headline** (700, `clamp(2.5rem, 1.6rem + 3.6vw, 4.25rem)`, 1.02, uppercase): h1 on inner pages.
- **Title** (700, 1.75rem, 1.02-1.1, uppercase): h2, board titles, footer name. h3 is 1.25rem, not uppercase.
- **Body** (400, 1.0625rem, 1.55): copy, capped at 62ch with balanced/pretty wrapping.
- **Label** (700, 1.25rem condensed, 0.04em, uppercase): buttons. Strip stops are 1.125rem at 600/700; field labels are Barlow 600 at 0.875rem.
- **Code** (500, about 0.9-1.06rem): short links and mono inputs.

### Named Rules
**The Board Voice Rule.** Condensed uppercase is for headings, buttons and route labels. Running text and form labels stay in Barlow sentence case.

## Layout

Single column, centred, max width 78rem (`--page-max`) with a fluid gutter `clamp(1rem, 4vw, 2.5rem)`. Narrow form pages cap at 34rem (Register keeps its confirmation code step on the same board); reading pages such as a single paste cap at 60rem (`medium`); there the meta line sits left with the view controls (language select for the owner, Rendered / Source for Markdown, Line numbers) right, wrapping under it when narrow. Page sections start 3rem from the navbar. Spacing is a 4px-based rem scale (0.25 to 4.5rem, steps 1-8); gaps inside panels are 1.5rem (2rem from 40rem up). The home hero is one column, then two columns (1.05fr / 1fr, headline left, Shorten board right) from 62rem. The logged-in Pastebin is a wide two-column workspace from 62rem (composer left and sticky, list right); logged out it is a single narrow board. The logged-in Shorten page follows the same workspace (the Shorten board left and sticky, the "Your links" board right, 1fr / 1.15fr), one column below 62rem, and a narrow gate board logged out. The logged-in Upload page follows the same workspace from 62rem (columns 1fr / 1.35fr, 2rem gap): the Upload board with the Storage board under it on the left, sticky below the navbar, and the "Your uploads" board on the right; below 62rem everything is one column, and logged out it is a narrow gate board. Rows inside the uploads board lay out by container query on the board (one line from 36rem of board width), not by viewport. ## Elevation & Depth

Depth is material, not atmospheric. Panels get a hard 0.4rem underside in `field-deep` (enamel thickness) plus a soft, offset cast shadow and a faint inset top highlight. Buttons carry a hard 0.25rem (0.1875rem small) underside in `action-deep` that compresses on press. The navbar rests on a 0.3rem solid route-green bar. There are no blurs, glows or translucent glass.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Enamel panel** (`0 0.4rem 0 var(--c-field-deep), 0 1.25rem 2rem -1.25rem <green cast>`): station boards.
- **Key underside** (`0 0.25rem 0 var(--btn-depth)`, hover `+2px`, active `0`): buttons.
- **Line bar** (`0 0.3rem 0 var(--c-line)`): navbar and open drawer.
- **Focus halo** (`0 0 0 4px line at 45%`): focused inputs.

### Named Rules
**The Thickness Rule.** Raised means a hard offset in a solid colour. A blurred shadow alone never signals elevation; only the panel's soft cast accompanies the hard edge.

## Shapes

Softly squared signage: 0.5rem on controls and thumbnails, 0.875rem on tickets, drop targets and inline detail panels, 1.25rem on boards, circles for stops, full pills only for small status chips and badges. Every panel and field has a 2px solid ink edge (`--edge`). Planned things use dashed strokes. The ticket slip has semicircular perforation notches. Boards clip to their radius and end their header in a diagonal kerb hatch.

## Components

### Buttons
- **Shape:** 0.5rem radius, 2.875rem tall (2.25rem small), condensed uppercase 700 label, nowrap.
- **Primary:** Signal Forest fill, enamel text, hard Deep Forest underside.
- **Secondary:** enamel fill, ink text, 2px ink border and ink underside. **Ghost:** transparent, no underside, Deep Meadow wash on hover.
- **Hover / Active:** lifts 2px with a longer underside; active presses down by the underside height in 60ms. Ghost does not lift.
- **States:** disabled is sunk enamel with hairline edge; loading shows a spinning ring in the label; error turns danger red with a 360ms shake. A form's submit key is _blocked_ until every field passes: drawn exactly like disabled (`aria-disabled`, no lift), but it stays focusable and clickable, and pressing it shows every field's problem, puts the cursor in the first one and, where the form flashes its key, shakes it red.
- **Held (rate limited):** after a rate limiter's 429 (vb2007/vb2007.hu-api#254), a form's submit key is truly disabled (drawn as disabled, not focusable, Enter in a field does nothing) and reads "Try again in 15 min", counting the API's `retryAfter` down the way the code keys do ("42 sec" under two minutes, then "15 min", then "23 hr 45 min", rounded up); its accessible name spells the units out where that keeps the visible label in it ("Try again in 15 minutes", but "Try again in 23 hr 45 min" stays as shown, `spokenWait`), and it's described by the message. It says "Try again" rather than its own verb, since "Delete account in 15 min" would read as a promise. It doesn't shake. The API's message stays where the form shows its errors, and stays for as long as the key is held, even after Cancel, Change, opening another form or typing a new code cleared the form's errors. The message carries the wait in words for screen readers ("Try again in 23 hours 45 minutes.", visually hidden, fixed when the hold starts), since the counting key is disabled and focus goes elsewhere; it isn't shown, so there's never a second, frozen number next to the counting one (only Log in's lockout message names its minutes, see GitHub Login). When the count ends, the message goes and the key comes back as it was (a red Delete or Unlink is red again). Focus that was on the key moves to the form's first field (the password where that's what was refused); a GitHub-only account's held Link GitHub key has no field, so its message takes the focus. Past the 15-minute window for wrong codes the hold is the daily cap of rejected codes, which counts per address, so Confirm code on the register step and Reset password add one line to the message: "A new code won't help: no code is accepted for this address until the wait ends." A hold is kept in the page only, so a reload ends it and the API's next 429 starts it again. It covers Log in (for the typed address, so another address can go at once), Register (the hourly mail limit, for any address), Confirm code on the register step and Reset password (for that address), the reset link step's Reset password (for the link on show), Send code on the reset's e-mail step (the mail limit, any address except one with a recent code, which goes back to its code step without asking the API), and on the account page Confirm code, Save password, Delete account, Continue to GitHub, Unlink GitHub and a GitHub-only account's Link GitHub. Keys with limits of their own (the resend keys, Change e-mail) stay usable.

### Inputs / Fields
Sunk-enamel fill, 2px ink border, 0.5rem radius, 2.875rem tall; Barlow 600 label above at 0.875rem, hint and error in 0.875rem. Hover and focus lift the fill to enamel; focus turns the border Signal Forest and adds the 4px line halo. Invalid state turns the border red with a bold red message. Fields are checked as they're filled in, never only on submit: a field shows its problem once it was edited and left, then re-checks on every keystroke, so the message goes the moment it's fixed. Problems more typing can't fix show while typing: too long, a forbidden character (a slash in a file name, a letter in a code), a confirmation that already differs where it's as long as the password. A field shows one problem at a time, in the order the API checks: a URL over 2000 characters that doesn't start with a known scheme names the format, and once it does, the message names its length and the limit ("This URL is 2,430 characters; the limit is 2,000."). A field merely tabbed through stays quiet. Left with a click, a field shows its problem once the click has landed, so the message can't push the clicked key away. The API's refusal of a field (a taken address, a wrong code) shows the same way until that field is edited. A mono variant is used for URL fields and the e-mailed confirmation code.

### Segmented Control
Joined keys for a one-of-few choice (a radio group). One sunk-enamel strip with a 2px ink edge and a 0.1875rem hard ink underside; keys are separated by 2px ink rules, labels in condensed uppercase 700 (1.125rem, 1.0625rem small) in Soft Ink, with an optional icon. Hover lifts a key to enamel and ink. The chosen key is Signal Forest with enamel text, like the current stop. Focus is the standard route-green outline. Legend above in Barlow 600 at 0.875rem.

### Select
A sunk field like the text input: sunk-enamel fill, 2px ink border, 0.5rem radius, 2.875rem tall (2.375rem small), native appearance removed and a chevron glyph on the right. Hover and focus behave like inputs (enamel fill; focus adds Signal Forest border and the 4px line halo). A list's sort is one Select labelled "Sort" (`SortSelect`), each option a field and a direction ("Name, A to Z").

### List Pager
Under a paged list, only once it has more than one page (`ListPager`): small secondary Previous and Next keys at either end, "Page 2 of 5" between them in Soft Ink 600 with tabular numbers. A key is disabled on the first or last page and while a page loads. It wraps on narrow boards.

### Two-click Delete
Deleting a row takes two clicks (`ConfirmDeleteButton` with `useConfirmDelete`): a small ghost "Delete" key arms on the first, turning solid red as "Confirm delete" (a primary key in the error state, since the ghost hover wash would dull the red); the second deletes, and the key reads "Deleting" with the spinner. It disarms after 4 seconds. There is one armed row per shared instance: the pastes list shares one, so arming another paste disarms the first, while upload rows arm on their own. A trash icon leads it only where the other keys in its line have icons too.

### Drop Target
A sunk-enamel panel (0.875rem radius, 2px ink edge, centred content) holding a stop: a 3.5rem route-green ring (5px) on enamel with the upload glyph in Signal Forest, a condensed uppercase title, a secondary "Choose files" key and a soft-ink hint. On drag-over the panel lifts to enamel with a Signal Forest border and the 4px line halo, and the ring fills Signal Forest, scales 1.12 and rises 0.25rem.

### Route Track (upload progress)
Each file's journey as a stretch of line: a departure ring, a 4px hairline rail, an arrival ring (0.875rem, 3px route green). The route-green run is full width and slides in by `transform` only. While uploading, a Signal Forest head rides the end of the run; waiting, finished and stopped tracks have no head. Done fills both rings Signal Forest; failed turns the run and arrival ring Fault Red; cancelled turns run and rings Soft Ink. A rejected file gets no track, only its red message.

### Upload Ticket
A finished upload in the queue gets a compact ticket slip: Meadow Field fill, 2px dashed ink border, 0.875rem radius, the one-line Upload Link and a primary "Copy link" key. It prints in with the same 720ms clip-path reveal as the full ticket. After copying a signed link, a soft-ink line (0.875rem, 600) under the link and key says when that copy expires ("The copied link expires at 14:32."), and goes once that time has passed. If the file was deleted or the link can't be renewed, the slip keeps the link as soft-ink text without the key, and says so in Fault Red ("This file was deleted, so its link no longer works." or "This link couldn't be renewed. Copy it from Your uploads instead.").

### Upload Link
A link on one line in the code face (500, 0.875rem). Shown without the protocol. The host and folders shrink first with an ellipsis (never below 4ch); the file part never shrinks, and a file name over 12 characters is shortened in the middle to its first and last four, as `6712…a3f1.png`. The URL without its query is the tooltip: a private link is signed (`?expires=…&sig=…`, vb2007/vb2007.hu-api#361), and the signature shows in neither, while Copy link and Open use the whole signed link. Enter it to register as anna; it works for 15 minutes.", naming the username the code will create, as every notice that leads to confirming does), which the code field also points at for screen readers; the "Confirmation code" field in the mono variant (numeric keyboard, one-time-code autofill) with a Soft Ink hint about the spam folder; a code that isn't 6 digits or that the API refuses (wrong, expired or used) as the field's own error, as on the form, and other failures (limits, mail, network) as an error Status Message above the key, which flashes red either way (past the code checks' limits, 10 wrong codes in 15 minutes or 20 in a day, when even the right code is refused, Confirm code is held for the address, e.g. "Try again in 23 hr 45 min", see Buttons); a full-width primary "Confirm code"; and under it a row with a small secondary "Resend code" key on the left and a small ghost "Change address" key on the right; both are disabled while a code is being confirmed, since its answer is about that address, and "Confirm code" is disabled while a new code is being sent, since that may replace the typed one. The address moves to the next line whole when it fits there, and only a longer one breaks, before a dot or the @.

After each send the resend key is disabled and counts down in its label ("Resend in 42 sec"), for a minute or as long as the API's `retryAfter` says, and focus moves to the code field. Every countdown key reads its wait the same way, in the site's English whatever the browser's language: seconds under two minutes ("60 sec", "119 sec"), then minutes ("57 min"), then hours and minutes ("1 hr 5 min"), rounded up so it never promises less wait than there is. While it waits, the key's accessible name spells the units out ("Resend in 57 minutes") where that still contains the visible label, so speech input can say what it sees; a wait of hours keeps the visible label as the name ("1 hr" isn't in "1 hour"). A waiting key is also described by the message of the 429 it waits out. The countdown is kept for the address for the rest of the browser session, so after a reload "Enter it" for that address picks it up instead of offering a resend the API would refuse. A resend refused by the one-code-a-minute cooldown shows a message that names no seconds (the label already counts them); one refused by a rate limiter (the hourly mail limit) shows the API's own message, "Too many e-mails requested: try again later.". That message is kept with the countdown, so "Enter it" after a reload shows it again next to the waiting key. Either goes when the countdown ends; any error also goes once the user types a new code, except why Confirm code is held (see Buttons). A code that a resend replaced is cleared from the field, so Confirm can't spend a try on it. The info message follows the code: "If a registration is waiting for …, a new code is on its way, and the one before no longer works." after a resend (the API answers the same whether or not one is), and "A new code couldn't be mailed to …, and the one before no longer works. Send a new one below." when the new one couldn't be mailed.

There are two more ways in. Under the form, next to "Already registered? Log in", "Already have a code? Enter it after filling in the form." ("Enter it" a button set like the link) opens the step for the typed address without sending anything (only once every field passes), e.g. after a reload: "Enter the code that was mailed to … It works for 15 minutes after sending, and registers you as anna with the password you entered." Registering the same address again within the minute opens it too, with the countdown the API gives: "Less than a minute ago, a code went to … It still works: enter it to register as anna." Only the API's cooldown does that: a rate limiter's 429 (the hourly mail limit) sent no code, so the form stays with the API's message.

Focus moves to the code field when the step opens and back to the e-mail field when "Change address" returns to the form, which keeps what was typed. A wrong code keeps focus in the code field. A username taken meanwhile returns to the form with "That username was taken in the meantime: pick another one. Your code works for up to 15 minutes after it was sent." under the Username field, which takes focus; the typed code and the resend countdown are kept, and while the address is the same the submit key reads "Continue": it (or "Enter it") reopens the step with that code instead of mailing another: "Your code for … works for up to 15 minutes after it was sent: confirm it to register as anna2." An address that already has an account returns to the form too, with "An account already uses this address. Log in instead (with GitHub if that's how you signed up), or reset the password from the log-in page." under the E-mail field. Either way the field takes focus and the Register key doesn't shake, since Confirm code was the key pressed. A confirmed code shows "Registration successful!" with a "Go to Shorten" key, and focus moves to that message (clear of the sticky navbar). If the account was created but the browser didn't keep the session cookie, the board instead shows an error Status Message ("Your account was created, but your browser didn't keep the session. Please check that cookies are enabled, then log in.") over a primary "Log in" key, focused the same way. Either way the form forgets what was typed, passwords included, and logging out (including from the navbar while on the page) brings back an empty form, never the finished code step.

### Password Reset
"Forgot password?" sits under the Log in password field as a small right-aligned link, before the submit key. It leads to the "Reset password" board, two steps on the same narrow board, each step's first field focused when it appears:
- **E-mail step:** one line of running text ("Enter your account's e-mail address to get a code and a link for a new password.", since the mail carries both), the E-mail field, an error Status Message above the full-width primary "Send code" key (held after the mail limit's 429, for any address, since nothing was sent: see Buttons), and two alt lines under the form: "Already have a code? Enter it" (a button drawn as a link, which opens the code step for the typed address without sending anything, e.g. after a reload) and "Remembered it? Log in" like Register's.
- **Code step:** the API's answer as an info Status Message first (it reads the same whether or not an account uses the address), then the address it went to as a fact (Soft Ink "E-mail" label over the address in ink 600, breaking anywhere rather than overflowing) with a small ghost "Change" key beside it (disabled while a code is being sent) that goes back to the e-mail step with the address kept, on a hairline divider. Then the Code field (mono, numeric keypad, one-time-code autofill, described by the message above and the hint "The 6-digit code from the e-mail. It works for 30 minutes."), New password with its "8 to 128 characters." hint, the field checks live (a 6-digit code, 8 to 128 characters) as each field's own error, and the API's errors (a wrong code, the mail limit) in one error Status Message above the full-width primary "Reset password" key, which is held for the address past the code checks' limits ("Try again in 23 hr 45 min" at the daily cap, where even the right code is refused; see Buttons). The e-mail step checks only the shape of the address (something@something), like Log in, so an older account's address still gets a code; Log in checks that and that a password was typed. Under the form, Soft Ink "No code yet?" beside a small secondary key that reads "Send again in 60 sec" and counts down (disabled meanwhile) after each code, or as long as the mail limit's `retryAfter` says ("Send again in 57 min", with the API's message in the error Status Message until the count ends), then "Send again". A resend changes the message to "If an account uses this address, a new code is on its way, unless one was sent in the last minute: then the one before still works." (the API answers the same either way), clears the Code field and puts the cursor back in it. The countdown belongs to its address and survives "Change" and a reload (kept in sessionStorage): sending to that address again before it ends returns to the code step without asking the API, saying "If an account uses this address, a code was sent to it in the last minute and still works." when there's no earlier message to show, and "Enter it" picks the countdown up too. A wait for the mail limit is kept with its message instead: coming back to it, also after a reload, shows the mail limit's error rather than claiming a recent code.
- **Link step:** the mail also carries a link, `/reset-password#token=…`. The token leaves the address bar and the history entry as soon as it arrives, on opening the page or in a tab already on it (where only the fragment changes, which also leaves whatever step was open), and the board skips straight to one step: the line "Choose a new password for your account. The link from the e-mail works once, for 30 minutes after it was sent.", New password (focused, with its "8 to 128 characters." hint and the same live check), the API's errors in one error Status Message, and the full-width primary "Reset password" key. A hidden username field carries the logged-in user's address when the link is theirs (and is left out otherwise). Under the form, two alt lines: "Link not working? Request a new one" (a button drawn as a link, which leaves the link for the e-mail step, focused, with the address filled in when the link was the logged-in user's) and "Remembered it? Log in".
  - **Refused link:** once the API calls the link wrong, expired or used, trying it again can't help, so the field and "Reset password" give way to its error Status Message over a full-width primary "Request a new link" key (focused and described by the message, leading to the e-mail step like the line above), with only "Remembered it? Log in" under it. Only the API's own text for a used-up link does this; a new password it refuses (its length rule) shows as the field's own error, focused, with the link kept for another one, and any other refusal stays in the error Status Message over the form.
  - **Too many tries:** the API's 429 doesn't use the link up, so its message gets a second sentence, "The link wasn't used up: it works until 30 minutes after it was sent.", and the form stays. Its "Reset password" key is held for the limit's `retryAfter` like any held key (see Buttons: "Try again in 15 min"), focus moves to New password, and the first alt line reads "Can't wait? Get a code instead" while it's held. When the hold ends, the message and that line go; leaving the link, or a new link in the same tab, ends it too.
  - **Malformed link:** a token that can't be one the API mails opens the e-mail step instead, its E-mail field focused, with "This reset link isn't complete or was changed: request a new one." as an error Status Message above its first line, until the code step opens. A fragment without a token leaves the page as it was.
- **Done:** the user lands on Log in with the API's message as a success Status Message at the top of the form, once. A user logged in as another account stays logged in: the board shows the message, then "You're still logged in as <address>. Log out to log in with the new password." (address in ink 600) over a primary "Log out" key, which brings back the form with the message still on top.

### Account Board
"Your account" holds a facts list (Soft Ink Barlow 600 labels over ink values, on hairline dividers), then two sections, "Log-in details" and "Linked accounts", each a heading in condensed uppercase (1.25rem) over one row per item, on a hairline divider like the facts and the Upload Row. A row has a 3.25rem tile like the upload thumbnail (sunk enamel, 0.5rem radius, 2px edge) with its mark in Signal Forest (a lock for Password, an envelope for E-mail, the GitHub mark for GitHub). Next to it sit the name in condensed uppercase (1.125rem), with a status badge beside it on the GitHub row, a Soft Ink note, and a small key.

The Password row's note says changing it logs out other devices; its secondary "Change password" key opens a form in place under the note, stretched to the row's width, and hides while it's open. The form is a 1rem grid of three password fields (current, new with the hint "8 to 128 characters.", confirm) and a wrapping row of a primary "Save password" (reading "Saving" while it works) and a ghost "Cancel"; Escape cancels too, clearing the fields and returning focus to the key. Field problems show under their field as they're filled in (the current password typed, the new one's length, the confirmation) and a submit focuses the first one; the API's refusal shows as an error Status Message above the keys. Too many wrong passwords hold Save password ("Try again in 15 min", see Buttons) and put the cursor in the current password; the account page's password checks (changing the password or the e-mail, GitHub, deleting) share one limit on the API, so each of their keys is held once its own try is refused, and the hold outlasts Cancel. On success the form closes and the API's message shows as a success Status Message under the note until the form opens again. An account without a password gets no key there, and a note pointing to the Password section below.

The E-mail row below it works the same way: its secondary "Change e-mail" key opens a New e-mail and a Current password field over a primary "Send code" and a ghost "Cancel", with the same field errors, API error message, Escape and focus handling. The address is trimmed and checked with the API's rule, and the current address is refused as a field error without asking the API. An account without a password gets no key there, only a note that changing it needs a password; the superuser gets none either, with a note that its address is set on the API. A confirmed address gets a "Confirmed" badge beside the row's name (the GitHub row's Linked pill); an unconfirmed one gets none, since older accounts were never asked to confirm, and it steps aside while a change waits, so it can't be read as being about the new address.

Sending the form doesn't change the address: the API mails a code to the new one. The form closes and the row shows, under the note and stretched to its width, an info Status Message "Waiting for confirmation:" with the new address in ink 600 (it moves to the next line whole, and only an address wider than the line breaks), then a code form: a mono "Confirmation code" field (numeric keypad, one-time-code autofill) with the hint "The code works for 15 minutes from when it was sent. Nothing yet? Check your spam folder, or get a new one with Change e-mail.", and a wrapping row of a primary "Confirm code" (reading "Confirming") and the secondary "Change e-mail" key. The field takes focus, and the waiting line describes it ahead of its hint, so a screen reader hears the address on arrival. The same state comes back on every load while the API has the change waiting. A code that isn't 6 digits (spaces are dropped; a letter shows at once) and a code the API refuses are both the field's own error, so the field is marked invalid and keeps the code and the focus; other failures (too many tries, no connection) show as an error Status Message above the keys. Too many wrong codes (10 in 15 minutes, or 20 in a day, when even the right code is refused) hold Confirm code ("Try again in 23 hr 45 min", see Buttons) with the code kept and focused; Change e-mail beside it stays usable, since asking for a new code has limits of its own. Once confirmed, the waiting line and code form go, the facts and the navbar show the new address, and the API's message shows as a success Status Message under the note, with focus on the key. A change already confirmed elsewhere (another tab or device) shows the same way, since the refreshed account has the new address: the code it refuses was spent on that change, so the success Status Message reads "E-mail address updated." instead of an expired code. A refusal that takes the waiting change away (the address belongs to another account or is reserved for the superuser, or the code expired with the page open) shows as an error Status Message in that same place instead, naming the address: "new@example.com wasn't confirmed." and the API's reason, or for an expired code "new@example.com wasn't confirmed: its code expired. Send a new one with Change e-mail." A refusal because the session ended (it expired, the password was reset, or the account was logged in or out elsewhere) isn't about the change, so it says nothing of it: the page logs out, as below.

There's no cancelling or resending on its own: a waiting change expires, and a new code is asked for with the form again. While a change waits, the form starts from its address with the hint "A new code replaces the one already sent." and focuses the password; Cancel or Escape goes back to the code. The API mails one code a minute per account, so after sending, and after a 429, the key is disabled as "Send code in 42 sec" (or "15 min", "1 hr 5 min" for a longer wait), counting down to the API's `retryAfter`; the count is kept for the session, so a reload doesn't spend a try. The cooldown's 429 reads "A code was sent just now. You can send another once the countdown ends." and clears with the count; it also refreshes the account, so a code asked for elsewhere (another tab) shows its waiting line. A rate limiter's 429 (too many wrong passwords, or the hourly mail limit) sends its wait too, but no code went out: the key counts that wait ("Send code in 15 min") next to the API's own message, which clears with the count, and the account isn't refreshed. Either 429's message is kept with the count, so opening the form again, also after a reload, shows it next to the waiting key. Only the cooldown's message tells the two apart (it starts "A code was sent just now"). A confirmed or dropped change ends the count, since the API then takes a new address at once. When the mail can't be sent, the form keeps the API's error and the row shows the change the API stored anyway.

The GitHub note names a linked account by its GitHub login in ink 600 ("You can log in with GitHub as @octocat."); the login moves to the next line whole rather than breaking at a hyphen. While GitHub isn't linked the key is a primary "Link GitHub"; once it is, a secondary "Unlink GitHub". On an account with a password both ask for it first, so a stolen session can't do either: the key opens a form in place under the note, like Change password (stretched to the row's width, the key hidden while it's open): one Password field (current-password autofill) and a wrapping row of a primary key and a ghost "Cancel". Linking's field has the hint "Confirms it's you. GitHub opens next, to approve the link." and its key reads "Continue to GitHub" with the GitHub mark, like every key that leaves for GitHub; unlinking's has "Confirms it's you. Afterwards you log in with your password only." and its key reads "Unlink GitHub" and turns solid red once the password is typed, like an armed Delete. The key is blocked until the password is typed ("Enter your password." once the field is left or the key pressed). Linking then leaves for GitHub: the key reads "Opening GitHub" and stays disabled from the request until the page is gone, since a second start would void the first, and the field empties as the browser leaves. Cancel is disabled and Escape does nothing too, until the browser should have left (5 seconds after the answer): a stopped or blocked navigation can then be cancelled, and the form opened again starts afresh. Leaving the page before the API answers drops the start, so the browser never goes on to GitHub from elsewhere. Coming back with the browser's Back key finds the form as it was left, with the field empty and the key usable again, and reloads the account, so a link made meanwhile shows. GitHub linked or unlinked elsewhere while a form is open closes it, forgetting the password, and focus in the form moves to the row's key, so the form can't turn into the other one by itself. The API's refusal (no password, a wrong one, too many tries, GitHub not configured on the API) shows as an error Status Message inside the form, announced with the field, and brings focus back to the field if it had left the form; the password stays typed. Too many tries hold the key ("Try again in 15 min", no GitHub mark, and Unlink no longer red; see Buttons), and for an account made with GitHub its "Link GitHub" key. Cancel or Escape closes the form, forgets the password and the refusal, and returns focus to the key. An unlink closes the form and moves focus to the success message at the top. An account made with GitHub links without a password: its note reads "Link it to log in with GitHub.", and its "Link GitHub" goes straight to GitHub, reading "Opening GitHub" (disabled) meanwhile, until the browser should have left, with a refusal as an error Status Message above the key, which takes focus back. Once linked, it has no password, so it gets no key and the note says GitHub stays linked while it's the only way to log in (the Password row above already says there's no password). Such an account gets a "Password" section under the logins (same condensed heading): a Soft Ink note on why to set one, New password (hint "8 to 128 characters.") and Confirm password fields, and a primary "Set password" key, left-aligned. Length and match are checked live like every field, the match again whenever the first password changes; API errors show as an error Status Message above the key. When the API wants GitHub to confirm it's them first, that's an expected step, not a fault: its message shows as an info Status Message and the key becomes a primary "Confirm with GitHub" link (GitHub mark) to the relink, which takes focus; the password is never kept, so it's typed again on return. Once set, the section goes, focus moves to the success message at the top, the Password and E-mail rows offer their keys and the GitHub row offers Unlink. A success Status Message at the top of the board confirms a link (the API returns with `?github=linked`, dropped from the URL like `?oauthError=`; for an account without a password it reads as the GitHub confirmation instead), a password set, or an unlink; a link GitHub turned away shows as an error there (a link started without the password, e.g. from an old bookmark, says to press Link GitHub on this page and enter it there). Opening the Change password, Change e-mail or GitHub form (or starting a GitHub-only link), submitting an e-mail confirmation code or setting the password clears these top messages first, so an older outcome doesn't stay above a newer action. The badge reuses the upload badge pill: Linked on Confirm Wash in Signal Forest with a check, Not linked on sunk enamel in Soft Ink with a cross.

The board ends with a "Delete account" heading (same style as "Linked accounts"), a Soft Ink note naming what goes with it (pastes, short links and uploads; it can't be undone), which for an account made with GitHub adds that GitHub confirms it's them first if they logged in more than 10 minutes ago, and a small secondary "Delete account" key with the trash glyph. The key opens the confirmation in place, as a sunk-enamel panel like an upload's details (0.875rem radius, 2px edge): the password field, or for an account made with GitHub a username field with the hint 'Type "name" to confirm.' (no autocapitalisation, matched exactly). Its "Delete account" key is blocked until the password is typed or the username matches, then turns solid red like an armed Delete; a username that doesn't match reads "That doesn't match “name”." once the field is left or the key is pressed; a ghost Cancel (or Escape) closes the panel, forgets the input and returns focus to the key. Setting a password while the panel is open switches the username field to the password field, which forgets the input and any refusal the same way. A refusal shows as an error Status Message inside the panel, announced with the field, and brings focus back to the field if it had left the panel (a clicked key disables while it works), so Escape still cancels; too many wrong passwords hold the key as "Try again in 15 min", no longer red (see Buttons); a deleted account goes to the home page, logged out. When the API wants GitHub to confirm it's them first (an account without a password, not logged in with GitHub in the last 10 minutes), its message shows as an info Status Message instead and the red key becomes a small primary "Confirm with GitHub" link (GitHub mark) to the relink, which takes focus; editing the username keeps it, since GitHub still has to confirm. The delete is remembered for the session for 10 minutes; Cancel or Escape forgets it, and so do the Password section's own "Confirm with GitHub" and the page coming back from GitHub with the browser's Back key. Back from GitHub within that time, the panel is open again with an info Status Message above the field, "GitHub confirmed it's you. Delete the account within 10 minutes.", which describes the field, and focus in the empty username field (typed again, never prefilled). It's said there, where the focus lands, and not again at the top of the board; it goes with the next try, Cancel or Escape. Without a remembered delete, the top message is the password's confirmation as before.

### Status Message
Tinted 2px-bordered strip with icon: success (Confirm Wash, forest text), error (Fault Red wash and text), info (sunk enamel, soft ink). Arrives with a short 280ms drop-in.

### Footer
Deep green band with a route line arriving from the left and ending in a terminus ring on the right edge, name in condensed uppercase, links in on-deep colour that brighten to bright route green on hover. The links are two groups, the open tools from `src/constants/stops.ts` and then the account links (Log in and Register, or Account), one right-aligned row on wide screens; when narrow they wrap between the groups, never inside one.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** put content on enamel panels over the green field, with a 2px ink edge and the hard thickness shadow.
- **Do** mark tools that do not exist yet with dashed strokes, soft-ink labels and the amber hatch; add a tool by appending a stop in `src/constants/stops.ts`.
- **Do** use condensed uppercase for headings, buttons and route labels, and Barlow sentence case for everything read in running text.
- **Do** write colour as tokens only; each is a single `light-dark()` declaration.
- **Do** keep focus visible: 3px route-green outline with 3px offset on everything focusable.
- **Do** honour reduced motion; animations here are decorative.
- **Do** draw progress and meters as route lines with stop rings (The Route Measure Rule).

### Don't:
- **Don't** build a dark-terminal homelab dashboard or a rounded-card SaaS page; both are rejected directions.
- **Don't** use gray or pure white/black; neutrals stay green-tinted.
- **Don't** use blur, glow or glass for elevation; depth is a hard offset in a solid colour.
- **Don't** use route green as text colour on the field; text is ink or Signal Forest.
- **Don't** use the amber hatch for anything but planned/in-progress.
- **Don't** draw progress or meters as filled capsule bars; they are route lines with stop rings.
